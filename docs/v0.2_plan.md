## What 0.2 is for

She's playing. 0.1 gave her a town, and after a few hours she and the user came back with a list:
things that look off, things that read oddly, things that are hard to reach, and one big one, "is
it fun?" (she was halfway through the critters in under an hour). 0.2 is the answer to that list,
plus a month-long Halloween festival for October, a new neighbour, a new place, and a fresh polish
on everything.

Two things shape how it's built:

- **Sessions are small.** Every session below fits one context window and ends with a green PR.
  A session can be started and stopped at any time; nothing waits on a session that isn't
  finished (`CLAUDE.md`, "Checkpoint as you go").
- **The code stays clean.** Every session runs the design review checklist, and the foundation
  sessions come first so new content is built on the fixed shape, not the old one. Two of the
  code's own "where it hurts" items (`docs/architecture.md`) are in the way of a new place, and
  they're fixed before one is drawn.

## The workflow for 0.2

- **`v0.2-dev` is the integration branch.** Each session branches from it, its PR targets it, and
  it's merged with a merge commit as soon as it's green. `main` (her phone) gets a **release** only
  when the user says so, as one PR from `v0.2-dev` to `main`, because Vercel deployments are
  limited. Suggested release points are marked ⬆ below; the user picks.
- **CI runs on every PR again.** The repo is public now (2026-09-29), so Actions minutes aren't
  metered: session A2 turns CI back on for drafts and restores Node 25's gates. The container still
  runs the whole suite, smoke included, before every push.
- **Vercel previews stay off** for `claude/**` and the dev branches (`vercel.json`).
- **Personal touches:** the 30 open questions in `docs/handoff.md` stay open, and 0.2's few are
  added there. The user answers when they like; every answer lands as a row.

## Principles for every session

The 0.1 principles hold (`docs/v0.1_plan.md`, "Principles for every phase"): rules in
`world/` and `systems/`, never in drawing code; time from the injected clock and the day key;
content as rows; saves versioned and migrated with a test; callers go to the service; nothing
punishes, expires or is lost (decision 11). For 0.2, three more:

- **A callout is fixed at its cause.** "Blue among them" is one line in the shop sheet that
  every outfit shares; the fix is a description per piece and a rule for which pieces recolour,
  not a special case for the jersey.
- **Content that grows gets a shape first.** Before the October festival adds weekly things, the
  calendar gets _events that span days_; before the new place adds stalls and games, the world
  gets _activities as rows_. A second festival or a second fairground is then rows.
- **The player can always reach the edge.** Every way out of a place is visible and tappable with
  the UI up. A session that touches the map or the HUD checks this in smoke.

### The design review checklist (in every session's PR)

The 0.1 checklist, unchanged: wrong layer? a file with a second job? new state saved, migrated,
tested, repaired? deterministic from the clock and day key? content as data, art in `sprites/`,
drawn by a test? the next three features? frame time or memory? what got harder?

## The big forks, settled

- **Fixes first, then October, then new content** (decision 132), and the sessions are small
  enough that the order is a preference, not a cut-off.
- **October is a festival that unfolds** (decision 133): the calendar shows the month as an event,
  and each week of October opens something new, so it can land week by week while she plays.
- **Fun is both the hunt and the checklist** (decision 134): critters get real rarity, seasons and
  places, so the last of them takes the year; and finishing a shelf earns something.
- **The UI is a frame** (decision 135): fixed bars top and bottom, the world drawn between them,
  never under them, so the edges of a place are always reachable. Every sheet is redesigned to
  the same frame, and nothing 0.1 could do is lost.
- **Sitting is just sitting, and the piano plays** (decision 136).
- **The new neighbour is a musician, and the new place is a fairground** (decision 137, Claude's
  call, open to change): Boothoven, a ghostly composer who moves in with a piano, and the Hollow
  Fairground beyond the graveyard, where the calendar's events happen and there's always a game
  to play.

## The sessions

Each is one PR into `v0.2-dev`, one context window, with the checklist. **S** is a short session,
**M** a full one. ⬆ marks a good moment to release to `main`, if the user wants one.

### A. Foundations (before anything new is built on them)

**A1. The ground in chunks (M).** The town's ground is one 1,280×1,600 canvas (7.8 MB), baked
once; a bigger map or a new place makes it worse, and the fairground is coming. Bake the ground in
chunks (say 8×8 tiles), draw only the chunks in view, and re-bake one chunk when its tiles change
(a holiday's decor, the frozen pond). Perf measured against 0.1's baseline. _Done when the town
draws from chunks with no visible seam, memory is down, and `npm run perf` says so._

**A2. Wiring, CI and the save chain (S).** `World.ts` is 884 lines, half of it handing each
service what it needs. Move the wiring into a builder (`src/world/build.ts`) so `World.ts` is the
class and its step. Turn CI back on for draft PRs and restore Node 25's gates (the repo is public;
decision 117 is superseded). Start 0.2's save chain: no version bump, a note in `migrations.ts`
that 0.2 begins at v25. _Done when `World.ts` is under 500 lines and CI runs on a draft._

### B. The quick fixes (what she hit first)

**B1. Rules that read wrong (S).** The frozen creek needs skates to walk on (without them, a
slippery line and she slides back to the bank), which makes the way on consistent. Planted mounds
centred in their bed. Toasts stay for as long as they take to read (by length, at least 3 s), and
a tap dismisses one. Farts dealt from a rolling hash so they don't fall in a pattern. Smoke covers
the creek. _Done when each has a test._

**B2. Copy that reads wrong (S).** A description per outfit (rows in `data/outfits.ts`), and
`fixed: true` on pieces that come in one colour (the Tigers jersey, the band tees' prints), which
the shop, closet and creator honour: no "comes in N colours" for them, no colour picker. The
hand's line on the quick bar says what it does ("Empty hands. Tap a bed and it gets what it
needs."). The Wes lines reworded. Gift items say what they are, not who'll give them; who gives
what moves to the relationships menu (U3). _Done when the dialogue test covers descriptions too._

**B3. What's new (S).** The first time she opens a new version, a card of patch notes in the
town's voice (the mayor's typewriter, since nobody has met them): funny, three to five lines, from
a `NOTES` row per version in `data/patchNotes.ts`; the version she last saw is kept per phone like
the dedication. Smoke covers it. ⬆

**B4. Selling one thing (S).** The sell counter sits under the whole of her bag, so with a full
bag a tap on a slot seems to do nothing: what it fetches and "Sell 1" are off the bottom of the
sheet. The counter moves where it's always in sight (the shop's head beside her Candy, or the
foot), and a tapped slot shows at once: its picture, name and count, what one fetches, **Sell 1**,
a − 1 + for how many, and **Sell all**. The bag's own card for a thing takes the same shape, so
the two read alike. _Done when smoke taps the last slot of a full bag and sells one without
scrolling._

### C. Reaching the edges

**C1. Ways in and out you can see (M).** Every exit gets a visible way: a worn path to the edge
and a signpost with a word on it (the art notes asked for words). Whisperwood gets a path that
winds through it past the creek and up to the clearing's gap, which is widened to two tiles and
marked by a lantern. The map sheet shows the ways out of the place she's in. _Done when smoke
walks to every exit of every place from its spawn by tapping only what's on screen._

### P. Getting about

**P1. A broom home, and out again (M).** The world map already goes straight to any place she has
found, but nothing takes her home, and the map's travel isn't something she'd guess. The portal is
**her broom** (question 50), an item on the quick bar: a tap and she hops on and swoops off, landing
on her home's mat. At home the broom hangs on a hook by the door (a piece of furniture, hers from
the day the broom comes), and walking up to it opens the world map to fly out to any place she has
found, or **back to exactly where she left** (the spot is kept, a save bump). The map's own travel
plays the same swoop, so all of it reads as one way of getting about, and its places say plainly
that a tap goes there. The broom comes by Agatha's letter (a new game in its first days, a save from
before on its first day of the version). Rules in `world.travel` (`home()`, `back()`), art in
`sprites/`. _Done when smoke flies home from the castle hill and back to the same tile._

**D1. More to say (M).** Every neighbour gets more lines in every band (hello, friend, close,
night) and a line per window, so a day's talks don't repeat. Cody keeps "babe" but says it in
about one line in four; the rest use her name or nothing. The dialogue test holds the babe rate
and that no line repeats within a day's talks. _Done when each neighbour has at least 8 lines a
band._

**D2. Talk that knows the day (S).** Lines that mention the weather, the window, what she's
holding, what she caught today, a happening on today, and her pet beside her, chosen before the
band's line when one fits. Rows in `data/smallTalk.ts`, chosen in `systems/dialogue.ts`.

### N. The garden grows

**N1. More places to grow (M).** Beds beyond the farm's first rows. The farm grows by
**extensions**, as her room does: a recipe (or Barty's offer, once he's a friend) that turns a
block of the map kept for it (like a newcomer's lot: grass until it's made) into a new row of
beds. Two places get a plot of their own: a shady patch by Whisperwood's creek and a lakeside
plot at Lantern Shore, each with a crop or two that grows a day faster there. And **planters**, a
piece of furniture with one bed in it, for her home or porch. `Farm` keys its beds by place and
tile (a save bump, the town's beds migrated under `town`); rain, sprinklers, the honesty stall
and the bed card work the same everywhere. _Done when a bed in each place grows, by a test, and
smoke plants one in the woods._

**N2. More to plant (M).** At least eight new crops from her touches (questions 13, 52): fun things
to grow (sunflowers, giant pumpkins, glow-in-the-dark gourds, a corn maze's sweetcorn) and food for
dishes the neighbours like (tomatoes, onions, garlic, herbs, an avocado bush, the game's own), each
crop feeding at least one new dish someone loves. Some grow a day faster in their season, never
slower out of it (decision 11). Her dishes (questions 19, 20): **spaghetti** and **chips and
guacamole** at the stove, the guacamole the night's snack now and then. Each crop is a row, a seed
on a shelf, a value the economy test holds, art in `sprites/garden.ts`, and someone who loves it.

### E. Candy

**E1. More ways to make Candy (M).** Today Candy comes from selling, the candy tree, the honesty
stall, the noticeboard, favours, visits and the lost things handed back. More, each one bounded
by the day or the window so no loop makes it from nothing (decision 128; the economy test grows
with each):

- **Making adds value.** A dish or a bracelet she makes from what she gathers and grows sells for
  more than what went into it; one made from what she bought never does.
- **The stall takes what she makes.** The honesty stall sells her dishes, bracelets and crafted
  pieces too, and a second shelf (a recipe) sells more each window.
- **Cobweb Corner's wanted list.** Three things a week it pays double for (a critter, a crop, a
  dish), rows dealt from the day key, shown on its counter and the noticeboard.
- **Candy saplings.** Now and then the candy tree drops a sapling; planted in her yard or on the
  farm, it grows into another candy tree (three at most) that fills each window like the first.

The fairground's games (M2) add prizes of their own.

### W. Her wardrobe

**W1. Bracelets on her wrist (S).** A bracelet she makes can be worn: `wrist` in her look, a **stack
on one wrist** (question 52; up to three), chosen from the bracelets in her bag. A worn one stays in
her bag, marked worn, and isn't sold or given until she takes it off, so nothing goes by accident.
Drawn on the doll's wrist in their beads' colours in every view and pose; the closet has a Wrists
row. The look's shape changes (a save bump). If it fits, a neighbour she gives one to wears it too.

**W2. A fuller closet (M).** More from the first day: a dozen pieces for every day (a hoodie,
leggings, a cardigan, overalls, a beanie, a skater skirt…; question 51 left the choice to Claude),
with her touches (questions 14, 34): **pink gardening gloves** and **her comfy tee**, oversized with
long sleeves. A save from before gets them too: the starter pieces are added to what she owns when a
save loads (pieces are only ever added, so there's nothing to migrate). Each with a description.
After K3, so they're drawn once, to its detail.

**W3. Cooler clothes to buy (M).** A pass over what the shops sell: new cuts the doll can't draw
yet (a jacket worn over a top, as its own layer; a corset top, a tulle skirt, a velvet dress,
platform boots, fishnet tights, a band hoodie), prints and trims, and a weekly **boutique** pick
on Cobweb Corner's clothes shelf with the best of them. Her answer to question 72 asks for more
than tees: a few fancy, pricey whole outfits (a spaceman suit) for the boutique, and more
Halloween costumes, which may go on the pop-up's Halloween shelf. After K3.

### D. Dialogue

**F1. Real rarity (M).** Four tiers (common, uncommon, rare, legendary) with real odds (about
12:5:2:1 by weight, and a legendary also needs its hour, weather or moon). Each critter gets a
season (months it's out) and stays in its places, so about a third of the Cabinet takes the year,
and a few need the fairground or the castle. The Cabinet shows a silhouette with a hint (where,
when, which season) instead of a blank. Existing catches are kept. _Done when a simulated year of
play at an hour a day fills the Cabinet in about ten months, by a test._

**F2. Reasons to come back (M).** Finishing a shelf (all the moths, all the fish, all of a
season) earns a reward and a letter from Wrapunzel, and the museum gets a wing per family that
fills as she donates, with a small display piece for her home when a wing is complete. Milestones
are rows in `data/milestones.ts`, checked by `world.milestones` from what the Cabinet holds (no
new save state beyond which rewards were given, save v26). ⬆

### G. Furniture she can use

**G1. Sitting (S).** A `seat` on a furniture row (where she sits, and facing which way): chairs,
stools, sofas, benches in town and the rooms. Walking up to one sits her down (a sit pose in
`systems/poses.ts`, drawn by the doll), the camera rests, and the next tap stands her up. Nothing
else happens; that's the point.

**G2. The piano (S).** A piano piece (`piano`, a recipe or on a shelf) that plays one of a few
tunes when she walks up to it, and the town's pianos (Boothoven's, the hall's) the same. Tunes are
`Tune`s in `audio/pianos.ts`. Other furniture that does something small on arrival (the music box
already does) is a `plays` on its row, so the next one is a row.

### H. Music

**H1. A tune for every place and window (M).** Background music by place (the town, the woods,
the shore, the castle, indoors) and by window (a morning and an evening variant), crossfading on
a crossing or a window's turn. Rows in `audio/music.ts`; `SoundBoard.setMusic` takes a key.
_Done when every place has its own tune and smoke hears a change on a crossing._

**H2. The fountain plays (S).** The pond's fountain plays a music-box tune while she stands by it
after dark, its lights pulsing to it. Halloween and Christmas get a tune of their own (the
festival's, J2). ⬆

### J. October: the Halloween festival

The town already dresses for Halloween 30 days before; what's missing is knowing it's a festival,
and things to do. October is four weeks; each session opens one, and they can land as October goes
on. Any of them left over lands the same way next year, by the calendar.

**J1. The festival on the calendar (S).** Events that span days (`span` on a calendar row):
"The Halloween Festival, 1–31 October" on the calendar sheet, the day chip and the title screen,
with a countdown to the 31st. A festival banner across the square, and the noticeboard's notes
about it. The shape for any later festival.

**J2. Week one: trick or treat (M).** Every evening in October, a knock at each neighbour's door
gets a treat (a Halloween sweet a day per neighbour, into her bag, kept in `Takings`), and a
