## What 0.1 is for

Version 0 is complete, but she hasn't played it yet, so 0.1 is what she'll open first. It proved
the loops (dress up, farm, collect, decorate, befriend), but the world is small and plain, the
buildings are dollhouses next to their owners, movement shimmers, some UI won't survive a bigger
closet, and there's one reason a day to open the game. Version 0.1 makes McFrancisVille a place:
drawn bigger and cuter, with somewhere to go, things to find, people with their own days, and a
reason to come back in the morning, the afternoon and the evening, and stay a while.

It is still her gift, but it is **not a world made only of her**: decision 15 (her likes are
scattered finds, roughly one in five) holds for everything new. Most new people, places, items and
events are the town's own, generic spooky-cute content, with her touches hidden among them.

## Principles for every phase

1. **Systems before screens.** A phase lands its rules in `world/` and `systems/`, with tests on a
   fake clock, before any HUD or art depends on them. A feature that only exists in the renderer
   or the HUD isn't done.
2. **No spaghetti** (decision 83). Each feature is its own module with one job, its own state,
   snapshot and tests, and it talks to others through the event bus or a narrow interface, never
   by reaching into another's fields. `Town` composes services; it doesn't grow new rules.
3. **One phase, one session, one PR.** Every phase is sized to fit a session with room to spare.
   A phase marked with a split says where it splits, and splits _before_ it starts if it looks
   big. Checkpoint as you go (`CLAUDE.md`): push after every step, keep "In progress" current.
4. **Review the design, every phase.** Each phase's PR ends with the design review checklist
   (below). Phases A, K and V are reviews in their own right.
5. **Data over code.** New content is rows keyed by id unions (`src/types/ids.ts`): a new zone,
   holiday, recipe, fish, event or neighbour is a row plus art, and a compile error everywhere it
   must be answered.
6. **Saves are versioned from 0.1's first day.** v0 saves are test saves (decision 80): 0.1 starts
   a fresh chain, and every change after that is a migration with a test, as before.
7. **Cozy rules still hold** (decision 11): nothing punishes, expires or is lost. A missed window,
   day, holiday or login is never a loss, only something waiting.

### The design review checklist (in every phase's PR)

- Does anything live in the wrong layer (rules in the renderer or HUD, drawing in the world)?
- Did any file grow a second job? (`Town.ts`, `main.ts` and the view are the usual suspects.)
- Is new state saved, migrated, tested, and repaired on load (never set aside over one bad id)?
- Is every rule deterministic from the clock, the window and the day key (nothing rolled while
  the game is closed)?
- Is new content data, with its art in `src/sprites/` and a test that draws all of it?
- What would the next three features in this area need, and does this shape allow them?
- Did the frame time or memory move against phase A's baseline?
- What did this phase make harder? If it closed a real alternative, it's a decision.

## The big forks, settled

**Codebase quality is the "backend"** (decision 83). No server: the game stays on her phone. The
investment goes into structure: phase A splits the 1,700-line `Town` into services before
anything new is built, and every phase is reviewed against the checklist above.

**The world grows as connected zones** (decision 78). The town becomes a bigger hub, joined at its
edges and doors to new areas, each small, cheap, and able to stay locked until something happens.
A world map travels between the places she's found. One giant map (too much memory on her phone,
hard to gate) and chunk streaming (too much engineering for this size) were weighed and set aside.

**The art goes bigger and cuter, now** (decision 79). Tiles double to 32 pixels, and characters are
redrawn at **32×48 in chibi proportions**: a big head with big eyes, a small body. That's more
detail for faces, hair and outfits than 16×32, and cuter than a taller 32×64. Buildings are 4–6
tiles wide (128–192 px) against a 48-pixel neighbour, so a door is a door. The redraw is staged:
phase C bakes the old 16-pixel grids at double size, so the game keeps working while each phase
replaces its part of the art. Every sprite stays a grid in code with palette swaps (decision 2);
phase C adds the tools that make bigger grids practical (shape and shading helpers, a script that
renders any sprite to a PNG for review). The first scale sheet goes to the user to judge on the
phone before the rest is redrawn.

**Three check-ins a day** (decision 81). The day has three windows: morning (from 5am), afternoon
(from noon) and evening (from 6pm). Gathering, the shop's specials, the noticeboard, critter spawns
and small events refresh per window, and neighbours do different things in each.

**Passive Candy** (decision 82). A candy tree by her house fills up a little each window, and an
honesty stall at the farm sells the crops she leaves in it while she's away.

## The phases

Each phase is one PR and one session. **S** fits easily, **M** is a full session, **L** has a
named split and becomes two sessions if it needs to. The order puts foundations first: nothing is
built on something that's about to move.

### Foundations

**A. Architecture review and foundations (M).** Write `docs/architecture.md`: the layers, the
seams, what each module owns, and where it hurts. Split `Town.ts` into a thin `World` composing
services (movement, gathering, farm, shops, home, neighbours, critters, pets, mystery, wallet),
each with its own state, snapshot and tests. Add the `Zone` abstraction (the town and her home are
the first two). Start 0.1's save chain (decision 80). Record a performance baseline on a phone-sized
Chromium run (frame time, memory). _Done when every test and smoke section passes on the new
structure._

**B. Movement and camera (S).** Snap the camera to her drawn position (the shimmer), a fixed
simulation timestep, an eased camera, and smoother paths (string-pulled A\*, fewer zig-zags).
_Done when smoke's frame dump of a walk shows no back-and-forth pixel._

### The art, bigger

**C. Art foundations at 2× (M).** `TILE_SIZE` to 32 and the pixel fit retuned; the legacy bridge
(old grids baked at 2× until replaced); the style guide (`docs/art_style.md`: outline rule, light
from the top left, palette ramps, dithering, chibi proportions); art helpers (shapes, shading
ramps, outlines from a mask); `npm run sprite` to render any sprite to PNG; a scale sheet (her, a
neighbour, a house with its 12-foot yard skeleton, a tree, tiles) in `?gallery`. The style takes
cues from the games she loves without copying any one, and draws spiders spooky-cute ("The look,
and the scale sheet" in `personal_touches.md`). **The user checks the scale sheet on the phone
before D starts.**

**D. Her and everyone at the new scale (L: D1 her, D2 everyone else).** D1: the doll's body
regions, hair styles and hand-drawn cuts at 32×48 (painted clothes follow, decision 27). Her
touches: idle poses (checking her phone, arms crossed), rocking out at big moments, the pink and
dark-brown split bob as her start, the rose chest tattoo and heart gauges (`personal_touches.md`,
"Her, drawn bigger"), and freckles and a nose piercing ("At the scale sheet"). D2:
neighbours, Wes and the Moon Pie Man (built from the doll's parts), pets and critters.

## What this plan does not do

It has no multiplayer, no monetisation and no server. It doesn't carry v0 saves forward (decision
80). Family as characters and the mayor's reveal (someone new, over a few months) are later work;
the calendar and zone systems are built so both can arrive as data.
