# McFrancisVille: plan for version 0.1

**Status:** draft, opened 2026-09-27. Waiting on the user's answers to the interview questions in
`docs/handoff.md` ("Still to put to the user"); the forks marked **(to settle)** below close when
they come in. Nothing is built yet. Update this line as the plan settles and as each phase lands.

## What 0.1 is for

Version 0 is complete and live. It proved the loops (dress up, farm, collect, decorate, befriend),
but the world is small, the buildings are dollhouses next to their owners, movement shimmers, some
UI won't survive a bigger closet, and there's one reason a day to open the game. Version 0.1 makes
McFrancisVille a place: bigger, with somewhere to go, things to find, people who have their own
days, and reasons to come back two or three times a day and stay a while.

It is still her gift, but it is **not a world made only of her**: decision 15 (her likes are
scattered finds, roughly one in five) holds for everything new. Most new people, places, items and
events are the town's own, generic spooky-cute content, with her touches hidden among them.

## Principles for every phase

1. **Systems before screens.** A phase lands its rules in `world/` and `systems/`, with tests on a
   fake clock, before any HUD or art depends on them. A feature that only exists in the renderer
   or the HUD isn't done.
2. **Her save is sacred.** She is playing a v0 save. Every change of shape is a migration with a
   test, and every phase adds her real save's shape (a fixture captured from a v0 build) to a
   round-trip test: load, migrate, play a day headlessly, save, load again.
3. **One phase, one session, one PR.** Each phase below is sized to fit one session with room to
   spare. A phase that grows is split before it's started, not halfway through. Checkpoint as you
   go (`CLAUDE.md`): push after every step and keep "In progress" in the handoff current.
4. **Review the design, every phase.** Each phase ends with the design review checklist (below)
   written into its PR. Three phases (A, H and P) are reviews in their own right.
5. **Data over code.** New content is rows keyed by id unions (`src/types/ids.ts`), so a new area,
   holiday, recipe, fish or neighbour is a row plus art, and a compile error everywhere it must be
   answered.
6. **Cozy rules still hold** (decision 11): nothing punishes, expires or is lost. A missed day, a
   missed holiday or a missed login is never a loss, only something to look forward to.

### The design review checklist (in every phase's PR)

- Does anything new live in the wrong layer (rules in the renderer or HUD, drawing in the world)?
- Did `Town.ts` (or any file) grow a responsibility that belongs in its own module?
- Is new state saved, migrated, tested, and repaired on load (never set aside over one bad id)?
- Is every new rule deterministic from the clock and the day key (nothing rolled while closed)?
- Is new content data, with its art in `src/sprites/` and a test that draws all of it?
- What would the next three features in this area need, and does this shape allow them?
- What did this phase make harder? Write it down in `decisions.md` if it closed a real alternative.

## "Backend" in a game with no server

There is no server today: the whole game runs on her phone, and saves live in `localStorage`
(decisions 5, 12). In this game, the "backend" is the simulation (`world/`, `systems/`), the data
(`data/`), and persistence (`persistence/`), and that's where 0.1 invests first: phase A pays down
the structural debt before anything is built on it.

A real server (cloud saves, content that updates without a deploy, sharing a town with Cody) is a
fork for the user **(to settle, question 1)**. It is deliberately not assumed: it would add hosting,
accounts or keys, and a way for her save to be lost that doesn't exist today. If wanted, it slots
in as its own phase after A, behind the existing `SaveService` interface (decision 5 kept that door
open).

## The two big forks, weighed

### A bigger world: how

| Approach                                                                                                                                         | For                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Against                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **One big seamless map** (grow `TOWN` to ~100×100)                                                                                               | No transitions; one mental model; nothing new in the engine                                                                                                                                                                          | The ground canvas (decision 23) grows to ~1600×1600 px (~10 MB on her phone); A\*, critters and neighbours all simulated everywhere; one giant map file to edit; hard to gate an area or hide a secret                |
| **Connected areas** ("zones": the town as a hub, with woods, shore, hills… joined at their edges and doors, the way her home already is a scene) | Each area small and cheap; areas can be **locked until something happens** (a natural home for "certain things must happen first"); secrets live in their own spaces; adding an area later is a row and a map, which is room to grow | A short fade at each edge; neighbours who walk between areas need a schedule-level model when off-screen (they're only placed exactly where she is); a little engine work (a `Zone` abstraction, travel, a world map) |
| **Chunked streaming** (one endless world loaded in pieces)                                                                                       | Seamless and scalable                                                                                                                                                                                                                | The most engineering of the three, for a game this size; every system must understand chunks                                                                                                                          |

**Recommendation: connected areas**, with the town itself grown too (about 50×64 tiles as the
hub), 4–6 new areas in 0.1, and a small world map (a sheet) to travel between discovered areas.
**(to settle, question 2)**

### The art: why the buildings look small, and what to do

She is 16×32 (decision 32) on 16-pixel tiles, and the houses are 3×3 tiles (48 px): her door is at
her shoulder. Stardew Valley, which she is drawn to match, has the same character size and puts
it in houses 5–8 tiles wide. The options:

| Option                                                            | For                                                                                               | Against                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Keep the scale; make buildings and props bigger and richer** | Every doll, outfit, critter, pet and furniture grid stays; buildings 5–8 tiles wide read properly | Buildings need redrawing (they need it anyway); the town needs more room (it's getting it)   |
| B. Double the density (32-pixel tiles, 32×64 characters)          | Much more room for detail and expression                                                          | Redraws every sprite in the game (thousands of lines of grids): effectively a new game's art |
| C. Smaller characters (16×24) in the current buildings            | Buildings "fit"                                                                                   | Undoes decision 32; outfits lose the detail she was redrawn for                              |
| D. Zoom out (see more of the world at once)                       | Cheap; the world feels bigger; a setting                                                          | Everything is smaller on a phone; tap targets shrink                                         |

**Recommendation: A, with a style guide.** Buildings grow to 5–8 tiles with deeper roofs, porches
and proper doors; each building gets its own exterior grid rather than a recolour (the three
houses share one today); the ground gets auto-tiled edges (paths, water, cliffs), clutter (tufts,
pebbles, mushrooms, fences, benches, signs, lamp posts), and small animations (water shimmer,
chimney smoke, swaying grass). A written style guide (`docs/art_style.md`) fixes the outline
rule, light direction, palette ramps and dithering, so every later sprite matches. A "more
stylized" look is welcome where it helps (bolder silhouettes, hue-shifted shading) without leaving
pixel art. **(to settle, question 3)**

**Movement.** The shimmer is a known bug: the camera and the sprites round to whole game pixels
separately, so she jumps a pixel back and forth as she walks (four device pixels on an iPhone),
and at four tiles a second each frame moves 1 or 2 pixels unevenly. Phase B fixes it (snap the
camera to her drawn position; step the simulation on a fixed timestep; ease the camera), and adds
smoother paths (fewer zig-zags, rounded corners).

## What else 0.1 brings (from the user's list, with Claude's additions marked ✦)

- **World:** connected areas, a bigger town, secrets (hidden paths, a locked gate, a buried
  thing, a critter only found in one place), areas and neighbours unlocked by what she does.
- **Art:** bigger unique buildings, every building enterable with its own interior, map detail,
  a style guide, smooth movement. ✦ Weather: gentle rain and fog days from the day key, which
  change which critters are out.
- **UI:** one collection component (scroll, sort, filter, search, "new" badges) used by the
  closet, bag, furniture storage, cabinet and recipes; a consistent sheet design; ✦ a quick bar
  for the thing she's holding (seeds, net, rod, can).
- **Time:** 2–3 check-ins a day: the day splits into windows (morning, afternoon, evening) and
  gathering, the shop's specials, critter spawns and small events refresh per window
  **(question 7)**. An in-game calendar (a sheet) of real holidays and town events.
- **Greetings and rewards:** the daily greeting varies (a holiday greeting; sometimes a pixel
  Tesla saying "red one!"; sometimes a reminder to feed her Pokémon **(questions 4, 5)**). Daily
  login rewards that count visits, never streaks (a missed day never resets anything). Passive
  Candy **(question 10)**.
- **Activities to do, not just check:** fishing, cooking, a farming revamp, and decorating
  interiors beyond her home. ✦ A town noticeboard of small requests that refresh each window.
- **People:** unique schedules, random and personal events, houses built after each neighbour
  with items she can only get through them, a small chance any neighbour farts, humans in town
  as well as monsters, and new neighbours arriving over time, some only once something has
  happened. ✦ Neighbours visit each other and her.
- **Holidays in town:** decorations, events, and dialogue that change with the calendar.
- **Fixes:** the dialogue that reads as if neighbours are eating Cody (question 6).

## The phases

Each phase is one PR and one session. Sizes are relative: **S** fits easily, **M** is a full
session, **L** is a full session only if nothing surprises. Nothing is L without a note on where it
splits if it has to. Phases are ordered so nothing is built on a foundation that's about to move.

### Phase A: Architecture review and foundations (M)

- A written review of v0's architecture (`docs/architecture.md`): the layers, the seams, what
  each module owns, and where it hurts. `Town.ts` is 1,700 lines and owns nearly everything; split
  it into a thin `World` that composes focused services (walking, gathering, farm, shops, home,
  neighbours, critters, pets, mystery), each with its own state, snapshot and tests, talking
  through the event bus.
- A save fixture from her v0 build, and the round-trip test from principle 2.
- A `Zone` abstraction (the town and her home become the first two), so phase C adds areas rather
  than special cases. No new content.
- A performance baseline on a mid iPhone (frame time, memory) to hold later phases to.
- Done when: every existing test and smoke section passes on the new structure, and the review is
  merged with its decisions logged.

### Phase B: Movement and camera (S)

- Fix the shimmer (camera snapped to her drawn position), a fixed simulation timestep, an eased
  camera, and smoother paths (string-pulling A\*, fewer zig-zags).
- Tap-and-hold to keep walking toward a finger ✦ (optional, if it tests well on the phone).
- Done when: a recorded walk across town shows no back-and-forth pixel in smoke's frame dump, and
  she feels smooth on a real iPhone.

### Phase C: The world grows (L; splits into C1 engine and C2 content if needed)

- Travel between zones (walk off an edge or through a door, a short fade), a world map sheet of
  discovered areas, and zone-aware saving (where she is, what's unlocked).
- The town grown to the hub size and re-laid out, keeping her house, farm and furniture exactly as
  she left them (a migration moves positions, never contents).
- Unlock rules as data ("the bridge opens once three neighbours are at three hearts").
- Two new areas to prove it: e.g. **Whisperwood** (a woods of old trees, mushrooms and a hidden
  clearing) and **Lantern Shore** (a lake with a pier, for fishing in phase K).

### Phase D: Art direction and the style guide (M)

- `docs/art_style.md`, a palette ramp pass, and the auto-tiling for ground edges.
- A reference sheet in `?gallery`: her, a neighbour, a building, a tree and a tile side by side,
  to judge scale on the phone before anything else is redrawn.

### Phase E: Buildings, exteriors and interiors (L; one building type per step)

- Every building redrawn at the new scale with its own exterior (the shop, salon, bakery and
  museum, the neighbours' houses), and every building enterable: interiors reuse her home's scene
  and furniture system, each decorated after its owner.
- Neighbours' houses carry items only friendship with them unlocks (a gift, or permission to buy
  a copy).

### Phase F: Map detail and life (M)

- Clutter and small details across every zone, animated water, smoke and grass, and ✦ gentle
  weather days.

### Phase G: The collection UI (M)

- One collection component: a scrolling grid or list, sort (newest, name, colour, kind), filter
  chips, search, "new" badges, and a detail panel. The closet, bag, furniture storage, cabinet and
  recipe book move onto it. A consistent sheet layout for every HUD sheet.

### Phase H: Mid-point review (S)

- Re-run the architecture review against what C–G built; performance against A's baseline; fix
  what drifted before the second half builds on it.

### Phase I: Time windows and the calendar (M)

- The day split into check-in windows **(question 7)**: what refreshes per window, derived from
  the clock as now (decision 4).
- A calendar system: real holidays (fixed and floating, like Thanksgiving) and town events as
  data; a calendar sheet; what's on today in the greeting.

### Phase J: Greetings, login rewards and passive Candy (M)

- The greeting system: weighted variants (holiday, the Tesla, the Pokémon reminder, Cody's usual)
  chosen from the day key, so each day's is stable.
- Login rewards by number of days visited (never a streak), a small gift on each window's first
  visit.
- Passive Candy **(question 10)**.

### Phase K: Fishing (M)

- A rod, fish as data (hours, zones, weather, rarity), a simple, forgiving catch (a tap on a bite,
  retryable forever), fish in the cabinet and museum. Her touches: ✦ a rare blue fish.

### Phase L: Cooking (M)

- A stove (at home, and in the bakery with Wrapunzel), recipes from crops, fish and gathered
  things, dishes that neighbours love and that give small effects (a walk-speed treat, a snack
  that lures a critter). Her likes: pizza, late-night snackies.

### Phase M: The farming revamp (M)

- Fix what's unintuitive **(question 8)**: likely a held tool (seed bag, can) from the quick bar
  so a tap does one obvious thing; visible states (dry, watered, ready sparkle); planting a row
  at once; a seed packet's days on the bed; sprinklers made at the workbench. Growth rules
  (decision 38) stay.

### Phase N: Neighbours with lives (L; splits into schedules and events)

- Unique schedules per neighbour (weekdays and weekends, windows, zones, visiting each other),
  personal events (a book club, a midnight bake, a spell gone mildly wrong), random small events,
  a small chance any neighbour farts (Cody already does).
- The dialogue fix (question 6).

### Phase O: New neighbours over time, humans among them (M)

- An arrivals system: newcomers who move in over the weeks, some only after something happens
  (an area opens, a friendship level, a holiday). Humans as well as monsters **(question 9)**,
  most of them the town's own characters, not people from her life.

### Phase P: Holidays in town, balance, and release (M)

- Decorations that go up and come down with the calendar, holiday events and dialogue, a balance
  pass across Candy, prices and rewards, and a final architecture review before 0.1 ships.

## What this plan does not do

It has no multiplayer, no monetisation, and no server unless question 1 says otherwise. It keeps
pixel art at the current density (unless question 3 says otherwise), and it never takes anything
from her save: a moved house keeps every piece of furniture in it.
